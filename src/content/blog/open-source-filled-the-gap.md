## The Classroom Limits

Bugema gave me solid theory. I can explain polymorphism, normal forms, and OSI layers in my sleep.

But when I joined my first open‑source project (**OpenMRS** – healthcare tech), I froze. I didn’t know:
- How to fork and keep a branch in sync
- What “CI is failing” meant
- How to leave a useful code review comment

## The Firehose of Learning

I picked a “good first issue” – fixing a typo in documentation. Simple, right?

That one typo taught me:

- **Git rebase** when my branch fell behind `main`
- **Pre-commit hooks** that kept failing (until I installed `black` and `isort`)
- **Draft PRs** – a safe space to ask “am I doing this right?”
- **GitHub Actions** – reading logs to see why tests broke

Two weeks later, my typo fix merged. I felt like I had climbed a mountain.

## What I Use Every Day Now

- **Issues & projects** – I manage my freelance work like an open‑source maintainer
- **Code review** – I learned to give kind, specific feedback
- **Documentation** – I now write READMEs that actually help people

## The Soft Skills I Didn’t Expect

Open source taught me:
- Patience with unclear instructions
- How to ask smart questions (`What have you tried?`)
- Gratitude – every maintainer who replied made my day

## To Recruiters

I’m not waiting for permission to learn. When the curriculum stops, I start contributing. You’ll get someone who knows `git rebase`, reads CI logs, and leaves better PRs than I found.

Let me show you.
