## The Myth of the Lone Genius

Before university, I imagined building software alone, headphones on, code flowing perfectly.

Then came the group project: four students, one API, and a shared Google Doc that became a warzone.

## The Real Challenges

**Schedules.** One person worked nights. Another had a job. I learned to use a shared calendar before writing a single function.

**Git conflicts.** Not the technical kind. The “you overwrote my work” kind. We settled on a rule: never push to `main` directly. Always branch. Always review.

**The quiet teammate.** Someone barely spoke in meetings but wrote the cleanest database queries I have ever seen. I learned to ask “what do you think?” directly to them.

## What Worked for Us

- A 15-minute standup (yes, like Scrum) every evening on voice call.
- A shared `CONTRIBUTING.md` (I wrote it – borrowed from open source).
- Rotating who presents. Everyone gets credit.

## The Result

We built a functional library booking system. But more importantly, three of us still collaborate on side projects. The fourth? They now lead their own open source initiative.

Group projects are not punishment. They are rehearsals for real teams.
